Why doesn't a satellite fall down?

It starts on my roof in Hayward, California.

Scroll

01 · What I noticed

My dish talks to the sky.

Every day the Starlink dish on our roof sends a signal straight up to a satellite.

I saw a photo of a satellite and it looked like it was just hanging there.

But a ball I throw always comes back down. So why doesn't the satellite?

02 · Follow the signal

Up to my satellite.

The beam goes up about 550 km. That is higher than a plane flies, but still very close to Earth.

Here it is. A Starlink satellite has a flat body and one big solar panel.

It looks like it is floating. It is not.

So why doesn't it fall?

My question changed from “why doesn't it fall?” to “why does it keep missing?” That is when the geometry showed up.

05 · The answer is one right triangle

Freeze it.

The blue line is the radius. It goes from the center of the Earth to the satellite.

r = 4

No gravity.

Without gravity it would fly straight, along the tangent. The radius and the tangent meet at 90°, so they make a right triangle.

d = 3

The long side.

The long side goes from the center to the end of the tangent.

√(4² + 3²) = √25 = 5

Gravity pulls it back.

So it would end up 5 − 4 = 1 unit higher. Gravity pulls it down exactly that 1 unit, and it is back on the circle.

5 − 4 = 1

Again and again.

Fly straight, fall back, land on the circle. Make the steps smaller and they turn into a smooth orbit.

always falling, always missing

It falls, but the Earth curves away just as fast. So it keeps missing.
